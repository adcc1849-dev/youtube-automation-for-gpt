# Episode 01 production brief — draft

**Editorial status:** draft; not approved.  
**Topic:** humanoid robots in factories and logistics  
**Working title:** How Humanoid Robots Are Learning to Work in Factories | Current Abilities, Limits & Future  
**Target runtime:** 8–12 minutes  
**Language:** English (US)  
**Audience:** US English-speaking AI/future-tech viewers  
**Angle:** real deployments versus marketing claims; demonstrated task capability versus general autonomy; practical limits and workforce implications.

## Proposed structure

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:40):** Ask what “working in a factory” really means; contrast a bounded task with general-purpose autonomy.
2. **What is genuinely happening (0:40–2:00):** Define humanoid robots and explain why companies are testing them in existing industrial environments.
3. **Case study: BMW Spartanburg (2:00–3:45):** Use BMW's and Figure's reported metrics only after checking both source pages; label them as company-reported.
4. **Case study: Leipzig pilot (3:45–5:00):** Explain the announced pilot and planned battery/component applications; confirm current status.
5. **Warehouse logistics (5:00–6:15):** Distinguish warehouse material handling from factory assembly; treat 2027 expansion as a plan, not completed deployment.
6. **The hard limits (6:15–8:30):** Evidence gaps around reliability, exceptions, safety, integration, costs, and intervention rates. Do not invent numerical benchmarks.
7. **Workers and the next few years (8:30–10:30):** Discuss task redesign, supervision, safety and training; include multiple credible perspectives before asserting labor impacts.
8. **Conclusion (10:30–11:00):** Summarize what is demonstrated, what is still uncertain, and what evidence viewers should watch next.

## Script requirements

- Write 1,200–1,800 words for the 8–12 minute target at approximately 150 words/minute.
- Include at least three named sections and a list of research claim IDs used in each section.
- All factual claims must trace to a source and be human-verified before approval.
- Clearly distinguish measured results, company claims, pilots, announced plans, and forecasts.
- No fabricated quotations, metrics, independent validation, or footage provenance.
- No reuse of source-page imagery unless separate reuse rights are documented.

## Thumbnail concept

Use original/generated illustration of a humanoid robot in a factory; prominent readable headline; avoid presenting generated imagery as documentary footage. Keep the title accurate and avoid implying that mass replacement has already happened.
